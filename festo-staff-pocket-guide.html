Basics

The know-how behind the product pages. Start here if pneumatics is new to you.

Read the label

Most enquiries are "another one of these". The label tells you what it is.

Part number and type codeThe part number is the 6 to 8 digit number on the label. Quote it to order exactly the same item. The type code describes the product: read it left to right.
Cylinder
DSBC-32-100-PPVA-N3
  • DSBCRange: ISO standard cylinder
  • 32Bore: piston diameter in mm. Bigger bore, more force
  • 100Stroke: how far the rod moves, in mm
  • PPVAOptions: cushioning and a magnet for sensors
  • N3Version. Copy it exactly
Push-in fitting
QS-G1/4-8
  • QSRange: push-in fitting (QSL is the elbow version)
  • G1/4Thread that screws into the product
  • 8Tube size it takes: 8 mm outside diameter
Tubing
PUN-H-8X1,25-BL
  • PUN-HRange: polyurethane tubing
  • 8X1,258 mm outside diameter, 1.25 mm wall
  • BLColour: blue
Cylinder sensor
SMT-8M-A-PS-24V-E-2,5-OE
  • SMT-8MRange: electronic sensor that fits the cylinder slot
  • PSPNP output. NS means NPN
  • 24VSupply voltage
  • 2,5Cable length in metres
  • OEOpen cable end. M8 or M12 means a plug
  • A, EOther options. Copy them exactly

Codes vary by range. If in doubt, type the full part number or type code into festo.com.

Jargon buster

The words customers use, in plain English.

Cylinders
Bore
Inside diameter of the cylinder (the piston size). Sets the force.
Stroke
How far the rod travels, in mm.
Single-acting
Air pushes one way, a spring brings it back.
Double-acting
Air pushes both ways. The most common type.
Cushioning
Slows the piston at the end of travel so it doesn't bang.
ISO 15552
A standard size, so cylinders from different makers swap over.
Piston rod
The bar that moves in and out and does the work.
Rod eye / clevis
Fitting for the piston rod to connect the cylinder to something.
WH
Distance from the end of the cylinder to the start of the piston rod thread.
Magnetic piston
A magnet inside the piston so a sensor can detect it. Needed for proximity sensors.
Side load
A sideways force on the rod. Too much wears the cylinder out.
Guided cylinder
Extra guide rods stop twisting and take side load (DFM).
Rodless / linear drive
A carriage runs along the body, with no rod sticking out (DGC).
Mounting
How the cylinder is fixed, for example foot, flange, trunnion or clevis.
Valves
Solenoid valve
An electrically switched valve that directs the air.
Valve terminal
Several valves on one base with one electrical connection.
3/2
3 ports, 2 positions. Turns air on and off, or drives a single-acting cylinder.
5/2
5 ports, 2 positions. Drives a double-acting cylinder out and back.
5/3
5 ports, 3 positions. The middle position can stop or hold the cylinder.
Port numbers
1 is the supply, 2 and 4 go to the cylinder, 3 and 5 are exhausts.
Monostable / bistable
Monostable springs back when the signal stops (single solenoid). Bistable stays where it was last switched (double solenoid).
NC / NO
Normally closed or normally open: what the valve does with no signal.
Coil voltage
The voltage that switches the valve. 24 V DC is the most common.
Manual override
A button to switch the valve by hand when testing.
Pilot-operated
A small air signal helps switch the valve, so the coil can be small.
Flow rate (l/min)
How much air the valve passes. Too low makes the cylinder slow.
Manifold / sub-base
A block that several valves bolt onto to share the air supply.
Mechanically operated
Switched by a lever, roller or button instead of electricity.
Air and connections
bar
Unit of pressure. Factory air is typically around 6 bar.
Air preparation (FRL)
Filter, regulator, lubricator. Cleans the air and sets the pressure.
Micron (µm)
Filter rating. The smaller the number, the finer the filter.
Port
Where the air connects on a product.
G thread
Parallel pipe thread, written G1/8, G1/4 and so on. The usual thread in the UK.
M5
Small metric thread used on small valves and cylinders.
R / NPT thread
Tapered threads. Don't mix them with G threads.
Push-in fitting
Push the tube in to connect, press the collar to release.
Tube OD
Outside diameter of the tube. Must match the fitting.
Tube ID
Internal diameter of the tube. Applicable with old style fittings where the tube fits over a barbed fitting.
Flow control
Restricts the air to set how fast the cylinder moves.
Silencer
Quietens the air as it exhausts.
Air quality (ISO 8573-1)
The standard grading for how clean and dry the air is.
Dew point / dryer
The temperature at which water drops out of the air. A dryer removes that water.
Condensate drain
Lets collected water out of a filter, either by hand or automatically.
Lubricated vs oil-free
Most modern parts run on dry air. Once a system has been oiled, it must stay oiled.
Soft-start valve
Builds pressure slowly at switch-on so the machine doesn't jump.
Shut-off / lockout valve
Cuts off and vents the air for safe maintenance.
Non-return valve
Lets air flow one way only.
Leaks
Escaping air, which costs money in compressor electricity.
Sensors and electrics
Proximity sensor
Clips into the cylinder slot and detects the magnet on the piston.
PNP / NPN
Type of output signal. Must match the controller. PNP is the most common in the UK and Europe.
M8 / M12
Standard round plug sizes for sensors and cables.
PLC
The controller that runs the machine.
Fieldbus
A network linking devices to the PLC, for example PROFINET or EtherNet/IP.
IO-Link
A smart connection that sends settings and diagnostics as well as the signal.
Reed vs electronic sensor
SME is reed and SMT is electronic, with a longer life.
Analogue output
A variable signal (4–20 mA or 0–10 V) rather than just on/off.
IP rating
Protection against dust and water. IP65 and IP67 are common.
2-wire / 3-wire
How the sensor is wired. It must match the machine.
Electric drives
Axis
One direction of movement.
Handling system
Multiple direction of movement. Defined by X axis (forward & back) / Y axis (left & right) / Z axis (up & down)
Spindle vs toothed belt
A spindle is precise and strong. A belt is fast and covers long distances.
Servo / stepper motor
A servo is fast and accurate, using feedback. A stepper is simpler and cheaper.
Motor controller
The unit that powers and controls the motor.
Encoder
Tells the controller where the motor is.
Repeatability
How precisely the drive returns to the same spot.
Homing
Finding the start point after power-up.
Process and vacuum
Quarter-turn actuator
Turns a process valve 90° open or closed.
NAMUR
A standard mounting pattern, so a valve bolts straight onto an actuator.
Positioner
Sets a process valve to any position, not just open or closed.
Media
The liquid or gas flowing through a process valve.
Vacuum generator
Makes vacuum from compressed air.
ATEX
Rules for equipment in explosive atmospheres. Always pass to technical support.
Spring-return actuator / fail-safe
A spring moves the valve to a safe position if the air fails.
Ball / butterfly valve
The common types of process valve.
Sensor box
Shows whether the actuator is open or closed.
Suction cup
The rubber cup that holds the part.
Vacuum level (-kPa / %)
How strong the suction is.
Air-saving function
The ejector switches off once the vacuum is holding.
Blow-off pulse
A puff of air that releases the part.
Ordering and service
Configurable product
Built to order from a set of options, so check the lead time.
Core range - Blue Star
Festo's fast-delivery range, marked with a blue star on festo.com.
Wear parts / repair kit
Seals and similar parts for refurbishing instead of replacing.
Successor product
The replacement for a discontinued part.
UKCA / CE
The conformity marks for the UK and EU markets.

No term matches. Try a shorter word.

When to hand over

You don't need to know everything. Know when to ask.

Pass to technical support when
  • 1The customer wants you to size or calculate something: force, speed or air use.
  • 2Safety is involved: emergency stops, guarding, or holding a load if the air fails.
  • 3It's a hazardous area (ATEX), food, pharma or cleanroom application.
  • 4The load is heavy or moves vertically.
  • 5You can't find a direct replacement for an old part.
  • 6The customer is thinking of moving from pneumatic to electric.
  • 7You would be guessing.

Pneumatic FAQs

Common questions about pneumatics and Festo. Tap a question to see the answer.

About pneumatics
1What exactly is pneumatic automation, and how does it work?Show

Pneumatic automation involves using compressed air to drive machinery and control various processes. It works by converting the energy stored in compressed air into mechanical motion through actuators, often pneumatic cylinders, which then perform tasks like lifting, shifting, and pressing.

2What are the key advantages and limitations of using pneumatic automation compared to other technologies?Show

Advantages:

Safety: Pneumatic systems are inherently safe because they use air, which is non-flammable and less hazardous compared to electrical or hydraulic systems.

Simplicity and Reliability: These systems are relatively simple to design and maintain, leading to high reliability.

Speed and Efficiency: Pneumatic systems can operate at high speeds, making them efficient for numerous tasks.

Limitations:

Limited Force: The force output is generally less than that of hydraulic systems, limiting their use in heavy-duty applications.

Air Quality Sensitivity: Pneumatic systems require clean, dry air to prevent wear and tear, necessitating additional components for air treatment.

Energy Efficiency: Compressing air is energetically costly, making pneumatic systems less energy-efficient compared to electric systems in some cases.

3In which industries and applications is pneumatic automation commonly found?Show

Pneumatic automation is commonly found in industries like manufacturing, automotive, packaging, and pharmaceuticals. Applications include material handling, assembly operations, and packaging machinery.

4What are the main types of pneumatic cylinders, and how do they differ in function and use?Show

Single-Acting Cylinders: Use air pressure to move in one direction and a spring to return to the original position.

Double-Acting Cylinders: Use air pressure for movement in both directions, allowing for greater control and force application.

5What are the different ways to control pneumatic valves, and what are their typical applications?Show

Valve control can be manual, mechanical, pneumatic, or electronic, with applications ranging from simple on/off control to complex speed and sequence control in automated systems.

6Why are air filters, regulators, and lubricators (FRLs) crucial components in a pneumatic system, and how do they work?Show

Air Filters: Remove contaminants from the air, protecting the system from damage and wear.

Regulators: Control the air pressure to ensure consistent operation.

Lubricators: Add a lubricant to the air to reduce friction within pneumatic components.

7Besides cylinders and valves, what other essential components might be included in a pneumatic automation system?Show

Besides cylinders and valves, essential components may include connectors, tubes, pressure switches, and sensors for monitoring and controlling the system.

8How can I design a basic pneumatic system to achieve a specific task?Show

Designing a basic pneumatic system involves defining the task, selecting appropriate components (cylinders, valves, FRLs), and ensuring proper air supply and control logic to achieve the desired motion or process control.

9What are the most important safety considerations when designing and using pneumatic systems?Show

Important safety considerations include using pressure relief valves, ensuring components are rated for the maximum system pressure, and providing adequate guards and safety interlocks.

10How can I troubleshoot common problems like leaks, slow movement, or component failure in a pneumatic system?Show

Common problems can often be addressed by checking for leaks, ensuring proper lubrication, and verifying that all components are functioning correctly. Recommended maintenance practices include regular inspections, cleaning air filters, and replacing worn parts.

11What are the recommended maintenance practices to ensure the smooth operation and longevity of a pneumatic system?Show
